Tracks

Two tracks. One goal.

Shark Tank is our incubator. The Workshop Track teaches the lab and translational skills behind it. Both exist to get Stanford science out of the lab and into companies.

Shark Tank

Shark Tank is BIOME's incubator. A cohort of student teams takes a scientific insight to an investable company, then pitches it live to a room of investors.

How Shark Tank works

Four phases. One cohort.

Each phase ends with a concrete milestone, so every team knows exactly what it has to prove next.

  1. 01

    Source

    Find a problem worth a company. Clinical need, a platform insight, or research that deserves to leave the lab.

  2. 02

    Validate

    Pressure-test it. Talk to clinicians, patients, and buyers. Map the science risk and the path to de-risk it.

  3. 03

    Build

    Generate the first proof. Prototype, run the key experiment, and shape the story around real data.

  4. 04

    Pitch

    Stand in front of the sharks with a thesis that holds up to diligence.

Questions

Asked often.

Who can join BIOME?

Any Stanford student, undergraduate or graduate, from any department.

Which track is right for me?

If you have a team or an idea you want to turn into a company, Shark Tank. If you want to build lab and translational skills, the Workshop Track. The two are designed to work together.

Do I need a startup idea?

No. The Workshop Track needs no idea at all, and the first phase of Shark Tank is about sourcing a problem worth building on.

Do I need a biology background?

No. Biotech companies need engineers, computer scientists, clinicians, and operators as much as bench scientists. Our teams are deliberately mixed.

What is the time commitment?

It depends on the track. The Workshop Track is lighter and the Speaker Series is drop-in. Shark Tank is a serious commitment for the length of the cohort.

When does the next track start?

Dates are announced to the mailing list first. Add your email at the bottom of this page.

I am an investor or a company. How do I get involved?

We work with funds, biotech companies, and Stanford programs as mentors, sharks, and sponsors. See the Partners page.
